For more than 2 years, I've been trying to figure out how people learn. 2 years ago, I thought I had the answer, so I made SciWeb, a website/platform that used AI to fulfill this goal. I didn't like it, so I reconsidered my notion of how people learn, rebuilt the site, and I've been doing this over and over for the past 2 years. I thought I wanted to run this like a tech company, so I recruited my friends interested in tech to join the team. People, including those on the team, often ask me, "So when are we going to launch" to which my answer is always, "soon", but then after I find a better way to do it and need more time to work it out and implement it, that "launch date" once again gets postponed. So SciWeb isn't so much a product as much as it is a vehicle for my discovery. I have learned a lot developing SciWeb. It's made me dig deep on the qualities of knowledge, the nature of intelligence, human and machine. It's also grown in scope; it started out as a study guide and note sharing platform: kind of like Saturn, where you just connect to people in your classes chat with them, and share class info. This was the initial "Web", connections between classmates with class resources and info. Then I expanded it into a learning system with the advent of ChatGPT, where it would take all of the worksheets uploaded into the class notebook, generate/extract questions from each worksheet, feed them to the user, and give them feedback. Then, I wanted to make a learning process instead of just an evaluation/question generator-- something that you could go into with no previous knowledge, and learn it all right there-- without the burden of having to upload class worksheets, just based on the core knowledge of the model. And I wanted it to be guided & structured. So I went through many pipelines. One had three parallel explanations of a concept, and you would highlight the parts that did and didn't make sense to you, and it would build a study guide from the parts that did make sense and keep generating these parallel explanations. Then I came across concept maps, which I thought beautifully deconstructed knowledge into digestable and manipulable pieces, which could be derived. I've always believed in constructivist learning, and I thought the flexibility of AI and concept maps combined might be able to make it happen. So I myself build a bunch of concept maps for different units of my classes, put them on the site, and the user would go through the nodes/concepts one by one, deriving them from their prerequisite concepts. But these hard coded concept maps were not personalized and while breaking my ideas into concepts felt satisfying for me, it turned into a guessing game for others. So I decided to keep the concept maps but to make the learning process more flexible whilst retaining visual structure. 

So in the current setup, there's a learning objective node on the map, and connected to it are key concept nodes, idea nodes, and question nodes. You click the learning objective node to record audio of you verbally answering the question(s), and then it takes that clip, transcribes it, parses it into nodes of those types, and generates nodes for follow up questions. All of this info goes through the concept map, so the idea is that there's an accessible, understandable, visual state of your learning progress and current understanding. The idea is that with minimal guidance, and freeflowing speech, it can help you connect, extend, and question your own present conceptions, something truely constructivist. Really, the goal is to help students discover knowledge through themselves. When I think about it, the only value of knowledge is that it can be derived. And the only way to further that knowledge-- whether you're a world-class researcher or a student that just wants an A+, is to be able to derive it. Now many people have tried to do what I'm doing in the past, but maybe not so many since powerful LLMs have come out. And the issue is I've devoted so much of my time in high school to this project that I need to actually turn this into a product that works. While I've gained so much from this endeavor, college can only see that if I make something groundbreaking. And I truly believe I have some excellent ideas here, and it's just a matter of piecing them together in the right way. I want to get to the core of how learning works best, and then go back through all of the previous versions of SciWeb and layer those applications back on.
